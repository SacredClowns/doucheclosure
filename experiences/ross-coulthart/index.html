Doucheclosure Experiences • Wing No. 11

The
Permit

A veteran investigative journalist reports that a recovered craft was too large to move — so rather than move it, they built a building over it. The review board accepts this completely and without reservation. Which is exactly the problem. Because if a building went up over it, then a building went up. And buildings do not go up in this country without a permit, a plan set, and five departments signing their actual names.

We want to be extremely clear: the board believes it. Nobody here is litigating the craft. The craft is stipulated. The craft is the least interesting thing in the story.

Here is the interesting thing. Somewhere in the United States, a general contractor opened a bid package, read the scope, priced the concrete, sharpened the number to beat two competitors, and won. He then mobilized a crew. He rented a pump truck. He ordered rebar by the ton and Port-a-Johns by the month and he stood on that pad at 6:15 in the morning with a coffee, looking at the thing he was being paid to build a roof over, and then he went home, and he had dinner, and he said nothing. Not to his wife. Not to his brother. Not at a single barbecue, for the rest of his life.

The board has reviewed the men in its own families and finds this superhuman. We know a man who could not keep quiet about a heat pump.

And before the concrete, before the crew, before the bid — the paperwork. Somebody sat at a municipal counter, or a federal equivalent of one, and filled out a form with fields on it. PROPOSED USE. STRUCTURE TYPE. SITE CONDITIONS. CONTENTS. EXPECTED DURATION. Somebody wrote something in every one of those boxes. And then somebody stamped it APPROVED.

That civil servant is, in the board's assessment, the most interesting government employee in American history, and there is no plaque, no hearing, no book. There is only a signature, in blue ink, on page four, and a beige building that has been standing there ever since, doing the single most successful cover-up in the world: being boring.

THE ZONING VARIANCE GRANTED 4–1 PROPOSED USE: STORAGE. STORING WHAT: ███████. One board member voted no. His stated objection was the setback. His actual objection is not in the minutes.
THE INSPECTOR PASSED, NO CORRECTIONS Walked the footing. Checked the rebar spacing. Checked the vapor barrier. Signed. Drove to the next site, which was a Wendy's. Ate a normal dinner. Slept.
THE PORT-A-JOHN MONTHLY SERVICE Every job site has one. Somebody serviced it on a route, on a schedule, with a truck, and invoiced for it. Somewhere there is a line item. There is always a line item.
THE PAINT 1 COLOR, 400 GAL Beige. Someone selected it from a fan deck. Someone held up two beiges and picked the more forgettable beige, on purpose, and that person understood the assignment better than any of us.
THE BUILDING C OF O ISSUED Still standing. Beige. Gutters. A downspout that needs attention on the northeast corner. The most important boring beige building on earth and it has a wasp problem.
