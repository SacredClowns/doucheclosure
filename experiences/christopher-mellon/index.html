The Christopher Mellon
Experience

His family name is carved into banks, universities, and an entire wing of the National Gallery. He served as Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense for Intelligence. And by his own public account, the videos that reignited the entire UFO question reached him in a parking lot — handed over like a car stereo, in office supplies. We believe every syllable. Our inquiry concerns a single word, and the word is VALIDATION.

WAS THE PARKING VALIDATED? WAS ANYTHING?

Sit with the scene. A scion of one of the great American fortunes — a man whose ancestors financed railroads, aluminum, and the concept of Pittsburgh — standing on painted asphalt between a Kia and a support column, accepting the biggest story in human history in a manila envelope. Manila. The envelope your landlord uses to return a security deposit, minus deductions. The fate of disclosure traveled in the same paper product as a middle school permission slip.

The intelligence apparatus of the United States maintains satellites that can read a wristwatch from orbit — and its most consequential transfer of the century was conducted at roughly the operational security level of splitting a Costco membership.

And no one — NO ONE — has answered the operational questions. Did he pay for parking? Did a Mellon feed a meter with pocket change like a mortal? Was there a stub? A stub is a paper trail. Did the source circle the lot first, or did he have the audacity to park in a good spot? Was the envelope at least sealed with one of those little string buttons, which everyone agrees is the luxury model? History records none of it. So the review board built the parking lot, and now you will stand in it.
