The Egg
Experience

Public testimony describes a craft shaped like an egg, recovered at night, carried out on a cable beneath a helicopter. We believe every single word of this. Which is why our review board has one concern, and it is the correct concern: that is not how you carry an egg.

Consider what was asked of that crew. The most significant object in human history — smooth, warm, reportedly humming — and the plan was to dangle it from a wire in the wind, like a piano in a cartoon, like an IKEA wardrobe over a canyon, like something you had no emotional relationship with whatsoever.

An egg is a promise. You do not swing a promise from a helicopter. You cradle it. You label it. You keep it at a stable temperature — a lesson this organization has been screaming about, in a different wing, for some time now.

The review board has therefore constructed a full-fidelity training environment. Fly the mission yourself. Feel the wind. Feel the swing. Feel the weight of a hum you don't understand on the end of forty feet of cable. And understand, finally, in your hands, what the review board has always known: this should have been a Yeti cooler and a forklift.
