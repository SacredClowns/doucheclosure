The Element 115
Experience

In 1989, a man described the fuel of interstellar craft: Element 115, stable, machined into wedges, five hundred pounds of it in government hands. In 2003, science finally synthesized Element 115 — and it fell apart in less time than a sneeze. We believe the man completely. Which leaves only one conclusion, and it is a logistics conclusion: the stable batch exists, and somebody lost the shelf.

500 LBS • ZERO LABELS • NOBODY SIGNED

Understand what the review board is saying. This is not a physics dispute. Physics did its part: it built the atom, watched it exist for eighty milliseconds — the lifespan of a regret — and wrote it down. No. This is a warehouse story. Somewhere under the Nevada desert there is a shelf, and on that shelf sits the power source of civilizations, and next to it — we know this in our hearts — is a box labeled "XMAS (MISC)" and a fax machine nobody is allowed to throw away.

Five hundred pounds of the most important substance in the universe, and the intake paperwork was never filed. The same government that issues a receipt for a $4.50 parking session. We checked. It does. We have the receipt.

And the hand scanner. THE PRONGS. A door that opens only when metal fingers press into your palm like a fortune teller with a security clearance. The review board has no joke here. The review board simply wants to know what the prongs knew, and why every subsequent government door went with badges, the coward's option.
